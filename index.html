Our mission

Every street has someone who lives on it

Most of what makes a town feel looked after is small. A drain that isn't blocked. A verge that's been cut. A corner without a pile of rubbish building up. None of it needs a budget or a meeting — it needs one person to decide that patch is theirs.

This isn't about showing off what you've done. It's about cleaning up, and keeping it clean, becoming a normal thing to do around here. A neglected corner tells everyone that nobody cares about this street. A looked-after one says the opposite — and that message spreads a lot further than the corner itself.

So claim a piece near your home and keep at it. The people who walk past it every day are the ones you're really talking to.

Why it matters

A few small things make a big difference

💧

Standing water breaks the tar

Water pooling in the road seeps into cracks. Traffic pumps it in and out until the tar breaks apart underneath — clearing a blocked drain near you is a five-minute job with a big payoff.

🌿

Clear paths keep people safe

Overgrown bush along a pavement hides pedestrians from drivers. Cutting it back makes the walk to work or school safer for everyone.

🤝

It's a team effort

Can't do it all yourself? Claim what you can, mark the rest as needing help, and someone with more time, tools, or a tractor can pick up the rest.

One thing before you claim something big. Your own pavement, verge or the corner at the end of your street — just get on with it. But a large neglected space belongs to someone: the municipality, a private owner, or a body corporate. For an open field, a dumping spot, a park or a stormwater area, have a word with your ward councillor or the municipality first. It's usually a quick yes, they can often help with bags or collection, and it means nobody can question what you're doing there.

Good to know

Common snags, and how people handle them

Am I allowed to clear it, or do I need permission?
Nobody minds a neighbour picking up litter or cutting back overgrowth on their own pavement or verge. A big neglected space is a different thing — it belongs to the municipality, a private owner, or a body corporate, and clearing it without a word can cause trouble.
Fix: For anything bigger than a verge — an open field, a park, a dumping site, a stormwater area — email or phone your ward councillor or the municipal offices and say what you'd like to do. It's rarely a problem, they'll often chip in with bags, a skip or a collection, and you've got an answer ready if anyone asks who said you could.
Where does the trash actually go?
Bagging it is only half the job — it needs to leave the area.
Fix: Bag as you go and drop bags at your nearest municipal collection point, or ask your household collection to take an extra bag on collection day.
People keep dumping there again
Clearing it once doesn't always keep it clear.
Fix: A visible "this area is looked after" sign helps a lot. Photograph repeats and report them to your ward councillor.
Working right next to traffic
Verges can be dangerous — cars don't always expect people there.
Fix: Face oncoming traffic, wear something bright, and work early or in quieter hours. Work in pairs where you can.
Thick bush hides snakes and sharp junk
Overgrown areas are exactly where these collect.
Fix: Wear boots and gloves, clear with a long stick first, and never clear thick bush alone.
Don't have the right tools
Not everyone has loppers or a wheelbarrow on hand.
Fix: Claim what you can — gloves and bags are enough to start. Mark the rest red so someone with tools can help.
