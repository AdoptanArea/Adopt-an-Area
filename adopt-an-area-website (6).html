Look after what's around you.

Adopt an Area is a simple way for neighbours to take care of the streets, corners and open spaces close to home — a bit of trash pickup here, some overgrown bush cleared there. Claim a piece near you, say what you're doing, and see everyone else doing the same, all on one map.

Why it matters

A few small things make a big difference

💧

Standing water breaks the tar

Water pooling in the road seeps into cracks. Traffic pumps it in and out until the tar breaks apart underneath — clearing a blocked drain near you is a five-minute job with a big payoff.

🌿

Clear paths keep people safe

Overgrown bush along a pavement hides pedestrians from drivers. Cutting it back makes the walk to work or school safer for everyone.

🤝

It's a team effort

Can't do it all yourself? Claim what you can, mark the rest as needing help, and someone with more time, tools, or a tractor can pick up the rest.

Good to know

Common snags, and how people handle them

Where does the trash actually go?
Bagging it is only half the job — it needs to leave the area.
Fix: Bag as you go and drop bags at your nearest municipal collection point, or ask your household collection to take an extra bag on collection day.
People keep dumping there again
Clearing it once doesn't always keep it clear.
Fix: A visible "this area is looked after" sign helps a lot. Photograph repeats and report them to your ward councillor.
Working right next to traffic
Verges can be dangerous — cars don't always expect people there.
Fix: Face oncoming traffic, wear something bright, and work early or in quieter hours. Work in pairs where you can.
Thick bush hides snakes and sharp junk
Overgrown areas are exactly where these collect.
Fix: Wear boots and gloves, clear with a long stick first, and never clear thick bush alone.
Don't have the right tools
Not everyone has loppers or a wheelbarrow on hand.
Fix: Claim what you can — gloves and bags are enough to start. Mark the rest red so someone with tools can help.
